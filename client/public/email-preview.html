WHAT HAPPENED

Sam opened by throwing out a wild stat: enterprise AI spending hit $180 billion this year, but nearly three-quarters of companies have not actually deployed a single AI tool internally. That gap between spending and doing became the theme of the whole episode.

That led Shaan to push his "boring AI" thesis. He argues the biggest money is not in building the next ChatGPT clone — it is in helping existing businesses automate the unsexy back-office stuff nobody wants to deal with. Think insurance claims processing, not chatbots.

The two went back and forth on whether AI coding tools will replace junior developers or actually create more demand for software. Sam thinks we will see 10x more software built, not fewer developers. Shaan was less sure and pointed out that the economics of hiring are already shifting.

They wrapped up debating whether distribution or technology wins in the end. Sam's take: the companies with existing customer relationships will beat the ones with the best models every time. The picks-and-shovels play is real.

KEY INSIGHTS
 Enterprise AI spending hit $180B but 70% of companies have not deployed AI internally
The biggest AI money is in automating back-office workflows, not consumer chatbots
Companies with existing customer relationships are beating pure AI startups
AI coding tools may create 10x more demand for software rather than replacing developers
QUOTE
Sam Parr on where the real AI money is:
““ "The best AI businesses right now are not selling AI. The real money is in helping Bob's Insurance Agency actually use ChatGPT."

🎧 THE TIM FERRISS SHOW

EPISODE
Morning Routines of Billionaires
GUEST
Tim Ferriss
Author and Investor
LENGTH
1 hr 12 min
LISTEN TO THE FULL EPISODE

🎧 Apple Podcasts · Spotify

WHAT HAPPENED

Tim started by asking each of his three guests — all billionaire founders — to walk through their first 90 minutes of the day in excruciating detail. What emerged was surprising: none of them start with email, social media, or news.

The first founder, who runs a $4 billion fintech company, starts every morning with a 20-minute "worry journal." He writes down every single anxiety on his mind, then rates each one from 1 to 10 on actual likelihood of happening. He says most mornings, every worry scores below a 3. The exercise clears his head before any real work begins.

Tim then shared his own updated morning stack: 10 minutes of meditation, a cold plunge, then 30 minutes of writing before touching any device. He emphasized the "before touching any device" part multiple times, saying it was the single change that had the biggest impact on his creative output.

The real punchline came when Tim pointed out that all three guests, without coordinating, had independently arrived at the same rule: never check email before 10am. Not as a productivity hack, but as a philosophical stance — your morning is the only time that truly belongs to you.

KEY INSIGHTS
 A $4B fintech founder starts every day with a 20-minute worry journal, rating each anxiety 1-10 on likelihood
Tim's updated morning stack: 10 min meditation, cold plunge, then 30 min writing before any device
All three billionaire guests independently decided to never check email before 10am
Morning routines are about protecting creative hours, not optimizing for productivity metrics
QUOTE
Unnamed fintech founder on reclaiming his mornings:
““ "I used to wake up and immediately reach for my phone. Now I treat my first two hours like a sacred creative window. My output tripled."
