Why they talked about it
Mel Robbins emphasizes the importance of environment in behavior change, referencing James Clear's "Atomic Habits" to support her argument. She highlights the concept that making a habit easier to engage with is crucial for success, stating that **"behavior follows the path of least resistance."** Robbins illustrates this with a personal anecdote about a rowing machine placed in an inconvenient location, demonstrating how physical friction can derail intentions. By simply moving the machine to a more accessible spot, the individual increased their chances of regular use, underscoring Clear's principle that visibility and ease can significantly influence habit formation.
