Page title: How PodCap Makes Money

BRAND REFERENCE
Before writing a single word of copy or building any UI, read BRAND.md in the project root. All colors, fonts, spacing, and components must follow the brand spec exactly. This is a light, white-first page. No dark backgrounds except where the brand spec explicitly permits them.

PURPOSE OF THIS PAGE
This page exists to do two things:

Tell users honestly and clearly how PodCap makes money
Protect user trust by making the wall between editorial content and commercial relationships completely transparent

Most disclosure pages read like legal boilerplate. This one should not. It should read like a founder explaining something they're proud of. Write it like a smart, direct person — not a lawyer, not a PR team.

PAGE STRUCTURE — build in this order
Section 1: Opening
Headline: How PodCap Makes Money
One short paragraph under the headline. The tone is: we know disclosure pages are boring, but ours is worth reading because the way we make money is actually unusual. Don't say that verbatim — imply it by just being direct and interesting from the first sentence.

Section 2: The Bookstore & Shop — Nothing Here Can Be Bought
This is the most important section on the page. Give it visual weight — use a left-border accent block (indigo, per brand spec) to call out the core principle.
The core message to convey:

Every book in the Bookstore and every product in the Shop was mentioned or recommended by a real host or guest on a real podcast episode. That's the only way anything gets in.
No brand, publisher, author, or PR firm can pay to appear in the Bookstore or Shop. There is no price. We don't offer it. We wouldn't accept it.
Rankings are determined by mention frequency across episodes and shows — not by commercial relationships.
When a book appears at the top of the Bookstore, it's because dozens of podcast guests recommended it. The recommendation belongs to your favorite podcasters, not to us.

Use plain, confident language. The word "never" is appropriate here and should appear at least once. Avoid hedging language like "we try to" or "we aim to."

Section 3: How Affiliate Revenue Works
Explain clearly:

When a user clicks a Bookstore or Shop link and makes a purchase, PodCap may earn a small commission through Amazon Associates or other affiliate programs
The price the user pays is identical — we never mark anything up
Affiliate commission rates have zero influence on rankings, placement, or what appears on the site
A product with no affiliate program still appears if podcasters recommend it enough
Affiliate revenue helps keep PodCap free

Keep this section factual and brief. No overselling, no guilt-tripping about supporting the site.

Section 4: Paid Advertising — Separate and Always Labeled
This section needs to draw a clear line between the affiliate content above and PodCap's paid advertising.
The message:

PodCap also runs paid advertisements. Brands can pay to have their products and services promoted to PodCap's audience.
These paid ads appear in the newsletter, on industry/category pages, and in personalized briefings — never in the Bookstore or Shop.
Every paid ad is written by PodCap in our voice, but it is always clearly labeled as sponsored. There is no ambiguity about what is an ad and what isn't.
Advertisers cannot buy editorial coverage. What PodCap writes about a podcast, episode, guest, or topic is never influenced by any advertiser relationship.
User trust is the most important asset PodCap has. Paid advertising exists to fund the product — it is never allowed to compromise the product.

Use a visual separator or callout to distinguish this section from the affiliate section above. The distinction matters and should feel intentional in the layout.

Section 5: Other Revenue Streams
Brief section, no fluff. Just a clean list:

Pro subscriptions (coming soon) — unlimited access, full briefings
Enterprise plans — custom intelligence products for teams
These are never affiliated with editorial decisions


Section 6: The Legal Part
This section can look slightly different — smaller text, more utilitarian. It's the FTC language. Include:

PodCap participates in the Amazon Services LLC Associates Program and may participate in other affiliate programs
Purchases made through affiliate links may earn PodCap a commission at no extra cost to the buyer
This page constitutes disclosure in accordance with FTC guidelines (16 CFR Part 255)
Affiliate relationships do not influence editorial decisions, rankings, or site content

Label this section clearly as the legal disclosure. Don't try to make it fun — just make it clean and readable.

Section 7: Contact
One short closing paragraph. If a user ever wants to know whether PodCap has a commercial relationship with anything they're seeing on the site, they can ask directly. Email: derek@podcap.io. We'll answer straight.
This should feel like a handshake, not a legal disclaimer.

TONE GUIDELINES — apply across the whole page

Write in first person plural ("we") throughout
Direct and confident, not apologetic
Plain English — no legalese outside Section 6
Short sentences and short paragraphs
The word "trust" should appear but not be overused — let the transparency itself do the work
Never use: "we strive to," "we endeavor to," "we are committed to," "it is our policy to"
The overall feeling should be: a company that has nothing to hide and knows it
