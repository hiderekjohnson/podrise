What top podcasters are saying

From Joe Rogan to Lex Fridman to My First Million, the smartest voices in podcasting keep coming back to this book — not as a history lesson, but as a live map of the present. The recurring theme: Orwell didn't write fiction. He wrote an instruction manual that nobody listened to.

What podcasters said about this book

JRE
The Joe Rogan Experience
Ep. #1891 · with Elon Musk
Nov 2023
Why they talked about it
Rogan and Musk used 1984 as the lens for a long conversation about modern censorship and platform power. The central argument: Orwell didn't write fiction — he wrote a warning about information control that Big Tech has quietly made real. They connected the book's concept of Newspeak — reducing language to limit thought — directly to how social media platforms narrow what ideas are amplifiable. For both of them, reading this book in their formative years was what made them permanently skeptical of any institution that controls what people are allowed to say.
MFM
My First Million
Ep. #512 · Books that changed our lives
Mar 2024
Why they talked about it
The hosts framed 1984 as required reading for anyone building a company in the attention economy. Their core takeaway: narrative control is a business moat, and Orwell mapped the mechanics of it 75 years before it became a trillion-dollar industry. They argued that founders who haven't read this book are operating blind — unaware of how the platforms they depend on are making the same moves Orwell described.
LEX
Lex Fridman Podcast
Ep. #369 · with George Hotz
Jan 2024
Why they talked about it
In a conversation about AI and surveillance, 1984 came up as the foundational text for understanding what Hotz called "the permanent record problem" — the philosophical danger of a world where nothing is ever forgotten. A society with perfect memory is one where change becomes impossible, because your past is always more accessible than your present.

Why they talked about it
Guy Raz included 1984 on his personal founder reading list — not for its politics, but for what it reveals about language as a tool of control. The discussion focused on doublethink: holding two contradictory beliefs simultaneously — a daily reality in corporate communications where leaders routinely say one thing internally and another externally.

About this book

Written in 1948 and published a year later, George Orwell's 1984 remains one of the most influential novels ever written. Set in a totalitarian future England, the story follows Winston Smith as he secretly rebels against the omnipresent surveillance of Big Brother. A searing indictment of authoritarianism, propaganda, and the fragility of truth.
