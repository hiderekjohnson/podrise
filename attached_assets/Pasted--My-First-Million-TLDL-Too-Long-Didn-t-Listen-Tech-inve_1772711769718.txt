🎧 My First Million

TLDL (Too Long, Didn’t Listen)
Tech investor Shil, who has deployed roughly $450M into startups, joined Sam Parr and Shaan Puri to explain how venture investing changes the way you see opportunity. Because investors can only lose what they invest but sometimes make 100x or more, they focus on finding asymmetric bets and increasing their “surface area”, meeting more people and exploring more opportunities to discover those rare outliers. He also shared the idea of “build your own yacht,” creating environments like dinners or events that attract relationships and opportunities. Later they mapped the AI race, where Shil believes most people will eventually rely on one primary assistant, with ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude currently leading.

What Happened

Sam opened by asking Shil what venture capital teaches about decision-making. Shil explained that venture investors look for asymmetric bets, where the downside is limited but the upside could be massive.

That led to a discussion about power laws, where a small number of companies drive most venture returns. Shil said the same pattern appears in life, a few relationships or opportunities often produce most long-term success.

To increase the odds of finding those outliers, Shil said he focuses on increasing his “surface area,” meeting more people and trying more things.

He also introduced the idea of “build your own yacht.” The metaphor refers to creating environments, like dinners, events, or content, that naturally bring interesting people together and lead to new opportunities.

Later, Shaan asked Shil about the AI landscape. Shil said he believes most people will eventually rely on one main AI assistant because context and memory will matter, with ChatGPT, Google’s Gemini, and Anthropic’s Claude currently leading that race.

The episode ended with a few quick stories and ideas, including Sam describing an attempt to buy the Breaking Bad house and turn it into an Airbnb, and a concept for a fitness brand designed specifically for older adults.

Quote

Shil on how venture capital reframes risk:
“In venture you can lose one times your money, but sometimes you win a hundred times.”
