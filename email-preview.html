WHAT HAPPENED

Sam opens by calling Jake "the most impressive founder under 30 he's ever met," which is saying something. Jake walks through the origin story — he was building internal tools for his university's student government when he realized every small business had the same problem: they needed custom software but couldn't afford developers.

He launched CloudStack on Product Hunt, got 2,000 users in the first week, and was profitable by month three. The conversation gets really interesting when Shaan pushes him on why he turned down the acquisition. Jake's answer: "I looked at every founder who sold early and asked if they were happier. None of them were."

The back half focuses on his growth playbook — partnering with accounting firms to white-label the platform, which now drives 40% of revenue. Sam calls it "the smartest distribution hack I've heard this year."

KEY INSIGHTS
 CloudStack processes $2B in annual transactions with only 47 employees
White-labeling through accounting firms drives 40% of revenue with near-zero CAC
Jake turned down a $50M acquisition because "no founder who sold early was happier"
The no-code market is projected to hit $187B by 2030

WHAT HAPPENED

The episode kicks off with Friedberg dropping a stat that stops the room: Google's Gemini is now processing 1.2 billion queries per day across its products, more than ChatGPT's entire monthly active user base. Sacks immediately pushes back, arguing that raw query volume doesn't equal product quality.

The real fireworks start when Chamath makes the case that Google's distribution advantage is insurmountable. "OpenAI has to convince people to open a new tab. Google is already the tab." Jason disagrees sharply — he thinks Google's enterprise AI is "embarrassingly behind" Microsoft's Copilot suite.

The second hour pivots to regulation. Sacks argues the EU's AI Act will inadvertently help Big Tech by creating compliance costs that crush startups. Friedberg counters with data showing European AI startups have actually increased funding by 30% since the Act passed, suggesting the "regulation kills innovation" narrative is overblown.

KEY INSIGHTS
 Google Gemini processes 1.2B queries/day across Search, Gmail, Docs, and Android
Chamath argues Google's distribution moat makes it "the most undervalued AI play"
EU AI Act compliance costs estimated at $2-5M for startups vs $50-100M for Big Tech
European AI startup funding increased 30% post-regulation, contradicting the "regulation kills innovation" narrative
